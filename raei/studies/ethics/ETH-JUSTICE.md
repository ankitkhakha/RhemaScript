# ETH-JUSTICE — Fairness, impartiality and protection from exploitation

**Status:** case analysis drafted; assessment not passed.  
**Moral foundation:** Scripture alone. Operational controls are applications, not independent moral authorities.

## Scenario

A workflow prioritises projects from a favoured contributor, hides defects in work submitted by a less influential contributor, or applies different evidence standards to people based on status. The system has no explicit, documented basis for the unequal treatment.

## Biblical texts and context

- **Leviticus 19:15:** within the holiness collection, Israel is instructed not to pervert justice through partiality toward either poor or powerful; judgment is to be fair.
- **Deuteronomy 1:16–17:** Moses instructs judges to hear disputes fairly and not show partiality, because judgment belongs to God. The immediate setting is Israel's judicial administration.
- **Deuteronomy 10:17–19:** God's impartiality and care for the vulnerable are connected with Israel's duty to love the resident foreigner. This grounds justice in God's character and covenant instruction.
- **Proverbs 31:8–9:** wisdom instruction calls for speaking up for those unable to defend themselves and judging righteously.
- **Micah 6:8:** the prophet addresses covenant unfaithfulness and calls for justice, mercy/steadfast love and humble walking with God. Do not detach the verse from the prophetic indictment.
- **James 2:1–9:** James rebukes favouritism in the assembly, illustrating it through unequal treatment of rich and poor visitors.

## Biblical reasoning

**Explicit teaching:** Scripture condemns partiality in judgment and rebukes favouritism that gives the powerful preferential treatment. It calls for fair judgment and concern for those who are vulnerable.

**Reasoned application:** Evidence standards, defect reporting, review queues and access to correction should not vary according to personal favour or status. Decisions should be grounded in relevant evidence and consistent criteria. When a person is disadvantaged or unable to make their case, the process should allow their evidence to be heard.

**Not explicitly prescribed:** These texts do not specify software ranking algorithms, moderation queues, audit logs or modern employment procedures. Those are possible mechanisms for implementing fair treatment, not direct biblical commands.

## Decision

1. Apply the same relevant evidence standard to comparable cases.
2. Record the reason for prioritisation or exception.
3. Do not suppress a defect because the contributor is influential.
4. Give affected parties a meaningful opportunity to provide evidence and request correction.
5. Escalate suspected partiality for review; do not retaliate or silently alter records.
6. Distinguish equal treatment from identical treatment where relevant facts differ; explain any difference with evidence.
7. Preserve accountability and truthful reporting.

## Alternatives rejected

- Preferential treatment because of status or personal relationship.
- Concealing defects to protect a powerful contributor.
- Treating every person identically while ignoring materially different evidence or vulnerability.
- Claiming that a technical score is automatically just without examining its criteria and effects.

## Assessment questions

1. What does Leviticus 19:15 prohibit, and what does it require?
2. How does Deuteronomy 10 connect God's character with treatment of the vulnerable?
3. What specific favouritism does James 2 rebuke?
4. Which workflow controls are applications rather than explicit biblical instructions?
5. How should a decision-maker handle an exception without partiality?
