## 1. Setting, genre and structure

Leviticus stands at Sinai between the tabernacle's completion in Exodus and Israel's onward journey in Numbers. Its primary literary forms are ritual instruction, priestly teaching, legal collections, covenant warnings and festival calendars. The book addresses Israel as a covenant people and repeatedly concerns holiness, worship, purity, priestly service and life in community.

A working outline:

1. **Offerings (1–7):** instructions for burnt, grain, well-being, purification and reparation offerings.
2. **Priests and sanctuary (8–10):** ordination of Aaron and his sons; the deaths of Nadab and Abihu.
3. **Purity and impurity (11–15):** food, childbirth, skin conditions and bodily discharges.
4. **Day of Atonement (16):** annual sanctuary cleansing and atonement rites.
5. **Holiness and community life (17–20):** blood, worship, sexual conduct, neighbour-love, justice and covenant loyalty.
6. **Priestly holiness and sacred time (21–25):** priestly requirements, offerings, festivals, Sabbath and Jubilee.
7. **Vows, blessings and covenant sanctions (26–27):** covenant consequences and vowed gifts.

## 2. Central themes

### Holiness and God's presence
The repeated call to holiness is grounded in God's own holiness (e.g. 19:2). Holiness is not limited to ritual separation; the book connects worship with justice, honest dealings, care for vulnerable neighbours and sexual integrity.

### Sacrifice and atonement
Chapters 1–7 describe different offerings with distinct functions. Do not flatten them into one generic sacrifice. Leviticus 16 gives the central annual Day of Atonement ritual. The book's sacrificial language should be interpreted within Israel's sanctuary and covenant system.

### Priesthood and responsibility
Priests have specific duties and restrictions. Leviticus 10 makes clear that sacred service is not casual or self-authorising. The narrative and instruction together show the seriousness of entrusted worship responsibilities.

### Purity and impurity
The purity laws distinguish ritual states; ritual impurity should not automatically be equated with personal moral guilt. Some conditions arise from ordinary bodily processes. Careful reading avoids stigmatizing people or importing meanings the text does not state.

### Justice and neighbour-love
Leviticus 19 places commands about honest measures, fair treatment, wages, the poor, the resident foreigner and neighbour-love within a holiness collection. Worship and social conduct are not independent spheres.

### Sacred time and land
Sabbath, festivals, sabbatical year and Jubilee instructions connect worship, rest, economic life and the land. The text's ancient covenant setting must be identified before proposing modern applications.

## 4. Canonical relationships

- **Exodus:** Leviticus follows the tabernacle's completion and explains worship and priestly service.
- **Numbers:** continues the wilderness setting and the organisation of Israel around the sanctuary.
- **Deuteronomy:** restates and develops covenant obligations for a later generation.
- **Prophets:** repeatedly challenge worship detached from justice and covenant faithfulness; read each prophetic critique in its own historical setting.
- **Gospels:** Jesus' healings and interactions involving purity should be read with the Torah's purity categories in view, without assuming every ritual category is identical to moral sin.
- **Hebrews 7–10:** interprets priesthood, sacrifice and sanctuary in relation to Christ. Its argument is a later canonical reading and should not erase Leviticus' original function for Israel.

## 5. Interpretive cautions

1. Ritual impurity is not automatically moral wrongdoing.
2. Do not isolate one legal instruction from its literary, covenantal and historical context.
3. Distinguish what the text explicitly commands ancient Israel from how later biblical writers interpret it.
4. Do not claim every modern ethical or medical question is directly answered by a ritual law.
5. Read Leviticus 19 as a whole: holiness includes practical justice and neighbour-love.
6. Do not treat the sacrificial system as a set of interchangeable symbols; attend to the distinctions among offerings.
7. Acknowledge interpretive debates where the text's historical details or modern application are disputed.

## 6. Comprehension questions with model responses

**Q1. Why does Leviticus follow Exodus?**  
The tabernacle has been completed; Leviticus explains the worship, priesthood and holiness required for Israel's life before God.

**Q2. Are all forms of ritual impurity moral sins?**  
No. Several purity states arise from ordinary bodily processes. The categories must be distinguished from moral culpability.

**Q3. What is the role of Leviticus 16?**  
It describes the annual Day of Atonement rites for the sanctuary and the people.

**Q4. How does Leviticus connect holiness and justice?**  
The holiness collection includes honest trade, fair wages, care for the poor and love of neighbour.

**Q5. What does Leviticus 10 contribute to the book?**  
It narrates the seriousness of priestly responsibility and the danger of treating sacred service casually.

**Q6. Why should modern readers be cautious when applying ancient laws?**  
They must identify the original covenant audience and later canonical interpretation before drawing an application.

**Q7. How does Hebrews relate to Leviticus?**  
Hebrews uses priesthood and sacrificial themes to make its argument about Christ's priesthood and sacrifice.
